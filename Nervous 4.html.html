The core law

One nervous system, two outflows

CNS (brain + spinal cord) → PNS (cranial + spinal nerves) → splits into Somatic (voluntary muscle) and Autonomic (involuntary organs) → Autonomic splits into Sympathetic and Parasympathetic.

Sympathetic

"Fight, flight or fright"

Response to stress. Ganglia mostly paravertebral (near the spinal cord). Short myelinated preganglionic fiber, long unmyelinated postganglionic fiber. One preganglionic neuron synapses on many postganglionic neurons → diffuse, widespread response.

Parasympathetic

"Rest and digest" — essential for life

Conserves energy, maintains baseline function. Ganglia close to or inside the target organ. Long myelinated preganglionic fiber, short unmyelinated postganglionic fiber. Usually one-to-one synapse → discrete, localized response.

Neurotransmitters

Preganglionic — both divisions

Acetylcholine (ACh) acting on nicotinic receptors of the postganglionic neuron.

Postganglionic — Parasympathetic

Acetylcholine (ACh) acting on muscarinic receptors of the effector organ.

Postganglionic — Sympathetic

Norepinephrine (NE) — except sweat glands and blood vessels of skeletal muscle, where it is ACh.

Adrenal medulla

Chromaffin cells release mainly Epinephrine directly into the blood, functioning as a sympathetic postganglionic "neuron".

Receptors

Nicotinic (ionotropic)

Ligand-gated ion channel → Na⁺/Ca²⁺ influx → depolarization. Found at all autonomic ganglia, the NMJ (different subtype), CNS and immune cells.

Muscarinic (metabotropic)

G protein-coupled. M1 = ganglia, M2 = heart, M3 = glands & smooth muscle, M4/M5 mostly CNS.

Adrenergic

α1 (A/B/D) and α2 (A/B/C) — 3 subtypes each; β1, β2, β3. NE and Epi can activate more than one type.

Functional rules

Reciprocal balance

The two divisions usually oppose each other in the same organ: if one inhibits an effect, the other enhances it.

Exceptions exist

Some effectors receive only sympathetic innervation: blood vessels, spleen, and piloerector muscles.

Sympathetic-dominant effects

↑ Heart rate & BP, bronchodilation, pupil dilation, piloerection, blood flow redirected to skeletal muscle, bladder neck contracts.

Parasympathetic-dominant effects

↓ Heart rate & BP, activates GIT motility & secretion, bronchoconstriction, pupil constriction, bladder/rectum emptying, bladder neck relaxes.
